Abstract — JANUS (Jupiter Amorum ac Natorum Undique 
Scrutator) is the multispectral camera that, on board of JUICE 
(JUpiter ICy moons Explorer) ESA mission, will prov ide high-
resolution images of Jupiter atmosphere and major G alilean 
icy moons surface: Europa, Ganymede, Callisto. Iner tial 
pointing, mosaicking, object tracking, stereo imagi ng for DTM 
(Digital Terrain Model) and the peculiar Ganymede L ibration 
measurements defined highly stringent requirement on the LoS 
(Line of Sight) knowledge of the JANUS OHU (Optical  Head 
Unit). Once in flight, the JANUS LoS is expected to  vary as 
consequence of thermal environment variations, in f act, orbit 
attitude change, spacecraft maneuvering and scienti fic 
payloads heat dissipations can modify the OHU tempe rature 
distribution. Optical elements translation and rota tion have 
been predicted through a STOP (Structural Thermal O ptical 
and Performance) Analysis, deriving a linear relati on between 
LoS, value and uncertainty, and OHU structure tempe rature 
gradient. This relation has been validated thorough  an on-
ground and in-air characterization test, the result s are 
presented in this paper. Test based coefficients al low real time 
and indirect LoS prediction & evaluation as functio n of OHU 
temperature sensor readings.  
Keywords— JUICE ESA mission, JANUS, Optical stabilit y, 
Performances, Line of Sight Space Instrument, High resolution 
camera 
I.  INTRODUCTION  
The JUpiter ICy moons Explorer (JUICE) mission will  
survey the Jovian system with a special focus on th e three 
Galilean Moons – Europa, Ganymede and Callisto – 
becoming the first SpaceCraft (S/C) to orbit around  the 
Jupiter moon Ganymede [1]. The main objective of JUICE is 
to address two key questions: what are the conditio ns for 
planet formation and the emergence of life? And how  does 
the Solar System work? The main components of the mission 
scientific strategy are: 1) to conduct a comparativ e study of 
Ganymede, Callisto and Europa 2) to provide a compl ete 
spatial-temporal characterization of the giant, rot ating 
magnetosphere, and of the meteorology, chemistry an d 
structure of Jupiter's gaseous atmosphere, and 3) t o study 
coupling processes inside Jupiter’s system and in p articular 
about the two key coupling processes within that system: the 
tidal effects that couple Jupiter with its satellit es, and the 
electrodynamic interactions that couple Jupiter and  its 
satellites with their atmospheres, subsurface ocean s, 
magnetospheres and magnetodisc.  
JANUS (Jovis Amorum ac Natorum Undique Scrutator) 
is the narrow-angle camera selected as the visible (from near 
UV to NIR) imager onboard JUICE. The JANUS instrume nt 
is composed by three units: 
- Optical Head Unit (OHU), mounted on the S/C Optic al 
Bench; 
- Proximity Electronics Unit (PEU); 
- Main Electronics Unit (MEU), located in the S/C vault.  
To meet the mission scientific requirements related to the 
Jovian Atmosphere investigations and Ganymede Libra tion 
measurements, the knowledge and characterization of  the 
Line Of Sight (LOS) of the OHU is crucial. In [2] a  
Structural-Thermal-Optical-Performance (STOP) analysis of 
the prediction of the LOS behaviour has been perfor med. In 
this paper the verification by on ground test measurements of 
this analysis is reported. 
II.  LOS CHARACTERIZATION WITH IN-AIR TEST  
The test focused on the main contributor of the LoS , that 
is the OMS (Opto Mechanical Structure) [3] deformation due 
to BW (Baffle Wall) and OW (Optical Wall) temperatu re 
gradient. 
According to [2], Y axis rotation is dominant wrt ( with 
respect to) X rotation, for this reason: 
  LoS = LoS Y (1) 
Knowing that all other sources of LoS variation are  of 
negligible effect, the test was conducted using the  OHU 
STM (Structural Thermal Model), including FPM (Foca l 
Plane Module) STM and FWM (Filter Wheel Module) STM. 
External harness and the S/C purging line were not installed.

A.  Concept 
Installed on an aluminum rigid interface plate (kep t at 
ambient temperature) the OW was heated to obtain a 
temperature gradient wrt the BW. This gradient lead ed to a 
differential contraction in X direction of the OW w rt BW, 
indeed causing a flexure of the full OMS (i.e. Y rotation). 
The temperature difference between OW and BW is 
named: Wall’s temperature gradient (ΔT Walls ). 
 
Fig. 1.  : OHU LoS variation due to Walls temperature gradient (spatial) 
B.  Assumption 
• Primary Mirror tilt = LoS of OHU  
The LoS main contribution is the global tilting of 
OHU structure. Being TEM (Telescope Module) 
stiffly connected to the OW, any rotation of the OW  
causes TEM  optical components rotation. So, the 
measurement of the M1 (Primary Mirror) rotation is 
equivalent to measure the whole TEM rotation, and 
of all OHU LoS variation. 
• Air environment and convection effects  
Involving only the reflection for theodolite 
measurement, the test was done in air.  
The advantages of testing in air were: a) the const ant 
accessibility to the OHU and the setup; b) the shor t 
transitory of the temperature distribution accelera ted 
thanks to convection; c) the absence of «chamber 
windows bending» due to vacuum that affect the 
theodolite measurement uncertainty (the LoS 
variation was previously tested in vacuum, but the 
depressurized condition induced the window to bend;  
such bending introduced uncertainty during 
measurement of more than 60 arcsec, larger than the  
measure itself).  
The disadvantage were: a) the convection constantly  
chilling the OHU so heating power to be injected was 
widely lost (i.e. setup required high Wattage); b) 
convection enhanced the hot spots location due to 
heating injection and reduced the uniformity of the  
temperature distribution. 
• Temperature stability in air similar to in flight 
The test data were collected when the variation of the 
M1 temperature wrt the OW temperature was slower 
than 2°C/h. From OHU in flight thermal analysis 
(Ganymede 500km orbit), the variation is predicted to 
be 1°C/h.  
So, the collection of data during in air-lab test w as 
done when thermal transient was comparable and any 
eventual different elongation, that could have induced 
a relative rotation between M1 and OW, was 
considered negligible wrt global OMS angular 
rotation (confirmed by analysis [2]).  
• Heating source location representativeness 
Test heaters installed were sufficiently representa tive 
of the hot spots expected from thermal analysis, se e 
Fig. 4. 
• Interface 
OHU interface was rigidly fixed on an aluminum 
plate kept at room temperature to avoid distortion due 
to differential thermo-elastic elongation. The plat e 
needed to be monitored to check its constant 
temperature all along the test. No external harness es 
were installed.  
• Theodolite measurement 
The rotation of the M1 was measured with a 
Theodolite, stable and permanently fixed on the lab  
floor. Since STM flat mirror was uncoated, an 
Optical Cube was glued on its surface, see Fig. 3.

V.  CONCLUSIONS  
Thanks to the in air characterization of the LoS as  
function of the Wall’s gradient, the proportional c onstant K 
has been measured and expressed with its uncertainty. 
From the data analysis collected during OHU STM 
thermal balance/cycle test, the error due to the PT 1000 
position representativeness wrt average of Walls temperature 
has been derived and considered for the uncertainty  
estimation. 
As result, when OHU will be in flight, the scientific team 
can use the telemetry of the two PT1000 sensors emb edded 
in OHU structure (at Baffle Wall and Optical Wall l ocation) 
to have a real time indirect measurement of the LoS  and the 
AKE.
