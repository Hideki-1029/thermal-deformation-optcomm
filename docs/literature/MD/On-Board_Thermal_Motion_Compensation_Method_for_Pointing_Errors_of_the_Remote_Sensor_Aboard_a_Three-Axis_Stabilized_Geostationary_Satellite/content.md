Hualong Hu®, Xiaochong Tong, Chunping Qiu®, He Li, Jiantao Fu, Yanfa Shang, and Jingtao Shi

Abstract—For earth observation from a three-axis stabilized
geostationary (GEO) remote-sensing satellite, a highly accurate
onboard thermal motion compensation (TMC) method for real-
time correction of the satellite sensor’s line-of-sight (LOS) point-
ing error due to thermally induced structural distortion internal
to the sensor during the diurnal cycle remains a global concern
to date. In this letter, we propose a novel TMC method for
GEO sensors. Compared with the traditional TMC methods, this
method has the following two advantages. First, we define the
LOS misalignment angle to model the comprehensive impact
of the sensor’s internal thermal misalignment angles on the
sensor’s LOS pointing behavior, which makes the optical path
modeling simpler and more generic. Second, we fit the repeatable

iurnal variations of the LOS misalignment angle based on long
time-series star observations; therefore, the calculation and the
use of the TMC amount are no longer limited to the assumption
that the sensor’s internal thermal misalignment angles rem
constant within a certain period of time, as in the tra
method. The proposed TMC method is theoretically app!
to all types of GEO sensors, including optical and microwave
sensors, and is verified by Fengyun-4A advanced geosynchronous
radiation imager (FY-4A/AGRI) TMC experiments.

The United States first applied the concept of image motion
compensation (IMC) on its second-generation Geostation-
ary Operational Environmental Satellite (GOES), GOES I-M
[8], [9]. The IMC system uses stars, landmarks, and range
observations taken over on day N to determine the satellite
orbit parameter set K and the sensor's optical LOS attitude
parameter set A and then uploads the (K, A) set on day
(N + 1) to generate the compensation amount required by
the sensor’s scan mirror gimbal angles to comprehensively
correct the sensor’s optical LOS pointing error caused by the
satellite’s orbital and attitude errors and the thermal distortion
of the scan mirror inside the sensor. The follow-on GOES
N-P satellites have upgraded the orbit and attitude models
used in the previous generation of IMC and replaced the earth
sensor with a star sensor for satellite attitude determination,
thereby further improving the sensor’s optical LOS pointing
accuracy [10]. The United States deployed a large focal plane
array imaging sensor, the advanced baseline imager (ABI),
above the third-generation GOES satellite GOES-R. Due to
the big difference between the optical pointing of the detectors
located in the center and at the edge of the ABI array, the
GOES-R satellite has abandoned the onboard IMC concept
and instead performed IMC on the ground by resampling
sensor observation data [11]. However, this ground-based IMC
approach will lead to a loss of radiometric accuracy and time-

Despite progress with on-board TMC methods, some short-
comings remain. First, traditional TMC methods generally
build a rigorous optical path model from the sensor’s internal
thermal misalignment angles (which are defined as the angular
deviations of sensor components with respect to their ideal
installation angles) to describe the actual LOS vector under
the effect of the sensor’s internal thermal misalignment angles.
This kind of modeling process is rather complicated and
often results in unstable estimates of the sensor’s thermal
misalignment angles due to the strong correlations inherent
in the rigorous optical path model. Furthermore, traditional
TMC methods need to calculate the TMC amount under the
assumption that the sensor’s internal thermal misalignment
angles remain constant within a certain period of time and
use the derived TMC amount to compensate for the ther-
mally induced LOS pointing error throughout this period.
In practice, on the one hand, it is necessary to consider the
determination of the length of this period, and, on the other
hand, it must be ensured that the satellite sensor can collect
enough star observations as well as accomplish the pre-planned
earth observation tasks within this period. Clearly, this harsh
assumption greatly limits the use of traditional TMC methods
on slow-scan GEO sensors.

This letter explores a novel TMC method for GEO remote
sensors. Its advantages are as follows. First, by equating
the comprehensive impact of the sensor’s internal thermal
misalignment angles on the sensor’s LOS pointing behavior
to the misalignment angle of the LOS vector in a proper
coordinate system [e.g., instrument coordinate system (ICS)],
a generic model characterizing the perturbing effect of the
s thermal distortion on LOS pointing behavior is
established, which overcomes the strong correlations between
thermal misalignment angles in the rigorous optical path
model. Moreover, long time-series star observations are used
to fit the repeatable diurnal variations of the sensor’s LOS
misalignment angle to establish the thermal motion model for
the LOS vector. During a daily cycle, the prediction of the
thermally induced LOS pointing error at any given time can
be easily achieved via the fitted LOS vector thermal motion
model, and the corresponding TMC amount can be calculated
without the assumption that the LOS pointing error due to
the sensor's thermal misalignment angles remains constant
within 15 min in [14], thus, theoretically closer to reality
than traditional TMC methods. Finally, the proposed TMC
method is verified by the onboard TMC experiments of FY-4A.
advanced geosynchronous radiation imager (AGRI). Since no
microwave sensors are operating in the GEO orbit to date,
the applicability of the proposed TMC method to microwave
sensors needs to be further verified.

IV. CONCLUSION

This letter proposed a new on-board TMC method applica-
ble for GEO remote sensors. Compared with the traditional
TMC methods, the advantages of the proposed method lie in
the following two aspects. First, we equated the comprehensive
impact of the sensor’s internal thermal misalignment angles
on the sensor’s LOS pointing behavior to the sensor’s LOS
misalignment angle defined in the ICS. This approach not
only overcomes the overparameterization problem inherent in
the traditional rigorous optical path model, but also makes the
optical path modeling process simpler and more generic. Sec-
ond, we developed a thermal motion model for describing the
repeatable diurnal variations of the sensor’s LOS misalignment
angle with the aid of a 6-order Fourier series and provided an
estimation method for the thermal motion model coefficients
(ie.、the Fourier coefficients of the sensor’s LOS misalignment
angle) based on long time-series star observations. Once the
Fourier coefficients of the sensor’s LOS misalignment angle
were determined, they can be substituted into the TMC model
to obtain the required TMC amount at any time. Thus, the
calculation and the use of the TMC amount are no longer
limited to the assumption that the thermally induced LOS
pointing error remains constant within a certain period of
time. By doing so, there is no need to consider the deter-
mination of the length of this period, which is theoretically
more rigorous. Finally, the real image data of FY-4A/AGRI
were used to verify the effectiveness of the proposed TMC
method.

6508405

In theory, the proposed TMC method is applicable to all
types of GEO remote sensors, including optical and microwave
sensors. However, constrained by the current state-of-the-art in
large-aperture antenna such as processing and manufacturing,
assembly and unfolding, 2-D mechanical scanning, and radio-
metric calibration, no microwave sensors are yet operational
in the GEO orbit. Therefore, the applicability of the proposed
TMC method to GEO microwave sensors still needs to be
verified in future research.
