Abstract: We propose a novel approach for recapturing targets as quickly
as possible after the space-borne ATP (Acquisition, Tracking, and Pointing)
system target loss in free-space laser communication. The approach uses
past angular information to predict the target trajectory. An optimized finite
memory filter is designed as the prediction algorithm. To obtain optimal
prediction performance, the designed filter determines the filter parameters
of different curve parts during offline training, and the parameters are
adjusted according to the curve characteristics during predictive filtering.
Simulation results indicate prediction accuracy above 0.1 degrees within 5
seconds. An experimental system was constructed in the lab to simulate the
reacquisition process using a dynamic target and a real ATP system.
Experimental results show that, compared with the classical orbit prediction
method, this approach can effectively yield shorter acquisition times.

1. Introduction

High capacity, high transmission rate, and excellent security make free-space laser
communication a promising prospective communication technology. Since 1994, countries
and institutions including Japan and ESA had successfully performed several experiments
demonstrating satellite-to-ground and satellite-to-satellite laser communication [1-3]. In June
of 2014, NASA transmitted a HD video “Hello World” from the International Space Station
to the ground station by using a laser beam in its optical communication project OPALS [4].
Accumulating evidence indicates that free-space laser communication is being used
extensively in a growing number of areas. This increasing popularity requires increasing the
robustness of the ATP (Acquisition, Tracking, and Pointing) system which is used to establish
high accuracy, high stability optical tracking-and-pointing link.

As applied to communication terminals, an important aspect of the ATP system's
robustness is the ability to quickly recover following optical link interruption. For laser
communications between LEO satellites and ground stations, limited by visual segments, the
communication time of each access is usually a few hundred seconds. Figure 1 shows the
duration of visual access of a LEO satellite-to-ground link in a time window of one month.
Figure 2 shows schematic of a typical laser communication workflow during one pass. During
the tracking process, satellite-to-ground optical link may be interrupted by optical signal
fading or even by completely blocked LOS (line of sight, hereinafter referred to as LOS), due
to atmospheric conditions such as turbulence, clouds, and rain [5] (Fig. 2), requiring link
recovery. As mentioned above, because the total communication time is short and the data
rate is high, even tens of seconds of communication interruption can significantly affect the
average data rate and the total data volume [5]. Obviously, link recovery time should be as
short as possible, which requires the ATP system - mainly the acquisition subsystem — to
quickly reacquire the target and establish the link.

A typical acquisition process comprises three procedures: search, recognition, and
execution. During the search step, the FOU (Field of Uncertainty, hereinafter referred to as
FOU, mainly caused by the inaccuracy of orbit prediction and satellite attitude measurement)
of a target is searched. Next, the light spot in the FOV (Field of View, hereinafter referred to
as FOV) is recognized to confirm the communication optical signal. Finally, an actuator is
driven by a controller to position the target optical axis at the FOV center. Accordingly, the
operation time of these three procedures constitutes the total acquisition time. Tan et al. [6]
and Zhang et al. [7] studied quick recognition of optical signals and moving characteristics of
driving motors, respectively, and they demonstrated that recognition time and execution time
both contribute no more than 100 ms to the acquisition time. In fact, most of the acquisition
time is devoted to the search procedure. For example, the maximal acquisition time of
STROPEX space-to-ground optical communication is 30 s [8] while for Artemis satellite-to-
ground optical communication the acquisition time is 51 s [9]. In order to shorten the
scanning phase of the acquisition process, scanning strategy optimization techniques have
been extensively studied [10-13]; however, only a limited improvement of acquisition time
has been achieved, because even an optimized scanning process still takes tens of seconds.
Another way to shorten the reacquisition time is by using prediction methods. Assuming
that the optical link interruption does not necessarily affect the relative motion of the target
beam, it might be possible to use the historical information for inferring the target beam
trajectory and to use the trajectory for open-loop pointing. By using the platform vibration
data and by assuming a constant change rate of the acceleration in a short period of time, Lee
and Ortiz [5] predicted the beacon movements on the camera when the signal faded (or was
blocked), and the maximal prediction time was 3 seconds (corresponding to a prediction error

1. Analyzing the prediction algorithm feasibility based on the angular information.

2. The principle of predictive filtering
2.1 Predictive filtering based on angular information

In general, two types of methods exist for predicting the two-axis gimbal pointing angle of the
space-borne ATP system during the dark period: 1) predicting in the Cartesian coordinate
system, and then calculating the pointing angle through coordinates transformation, 2)
predicting the angle directly in the spherical coordinate system [14]. Because the first method
requires the range information between the satellite and the ground station, and because the
space-borne ATP system usually has no access to precise range data, the second method,
which only requires angle information of the ATP system, is adopted for predicting. As
shown in Fig. 3, the LEO satellite’s pointing angle to the ground station, as well as its first

2.2 N-points finite memory filter

The key component of predicting is an appropriate predictive filtering algorithm. The
algorithms commonly used in optoelectronic tracking systems include a-B-y filters, Kalman

filters and N-points finite memory filters [17]. These are often used to improve the tracking
accuracy [18], forecast the radar-pointing angle [19], and predict the target trajectory [20]. In
this paper, considering the fact that the angular motion model of the space-borne ATP system
is unknown, we adopted the N-points finite memory filter as the basic prediction algorithm,
while the other two kinds of filters could be alternatives [21,22]. Next, the basic concept of N-
points finite memory filter will be briefly introduced.

The N-points finite memory filter uses the latest N points of data, which contain
measurement noise, to estimate the current and future data, ensuring a minimal mean squared
error [17]. This filter is also known as the polynomial filter, because polynomial curve fitting
is often used in this method [23].

3. Design and simulation of the optimized finite memory filter
3.1 The finite memory filter problem

The number of memory points and the function class are key determinants of finite memory
filters prediction performance. Using more points confers stronger ability to filter the random
disturbances; however, the effect of new data will be weakened, resulting in a reduced ability
to respond to mobility. Using fewer points increases the randomness and may cause instability
[17]. The function class determines the fitting curve type; for polynomial fitting, it mainly
refers to the polynomial order. Using higher-order polynomials can yield more flexural
curves; however, an improperly high order may result in extra error for trajectories close to
straight lines. Further, high order polynomials increase the calculation complexity, and
sometimes yield matrices with ill-conditioned coefficients. Thus, the selection of a proper
order should be based on the curve characteristics.

As shown in Fig. 3, the pointing angle curve from the LEO satellite to a ground station has
unique features. The lowest angular mobility in the curve appears at both ends because of the
longest range; the mobility is much higher in the middle, where the angular velocity and
acceleration attain larger values. By using simulations, we found that for this angle curve
type, there is a problem of parameter adaptability for the finite memory filter. That is, if a
fixed value parameter is applied, it is difficult to ensure the prediction effects on different
curve parts. To illustrate this let us consider the following example. Let us assume there are
two different sets of filter parameters, Pl and P2:

Figure 7 shows the flow chart of fast reacquisition including the optimized finite memory
filter algorithm. After the initial acquisition, the target is stably tracked. During the tracking
process, the ATP system automatically records a certain length (greater than or equal to
Nau, ) of miss distance data and measured angle data. The system then updates and stores the
true pointing angle after aligning and combining the two data types. The camera’s light spot
centroid data is used to determine whether the LOS is blocked. If the LOS is not blocked, the
above process is continued; otherwise, predictive filtering is performed by using the
optimized finite memory filter, and open-loop pointing is executed according to the predicted
angle. The prediction and pointing process will last for X seconds at most, where the value of
X depends on the prediction performance and the system FOV. During this process, if the
LOS is recovered and the prediction and pointing is accurate enough, the target will directly
reappear in the FOV, then the search process can be shortened, and fast reacquisition is
achieved.

However, if the prediction lasts for X seconds and fast reacquisition is not yet completed,
the system should be switched to scan strategy to complete the reacquisition anyway. The
scan strategy here might differ from the initial acquisition because it can still take advantage
of the prediction results. In other words, the prediction error might still be smaller than the
FOU, even though it exceeds the FOV. Thus, a specially designed scan strategy will possibly
improve the reacquisition process further, but it will not be discussed in detail in this paper.

In real-life application scenarios, the ATP system may encounter intermittent blockade of
LOS during dark periods, which will affect the valid data length of the filter, so we take some
measures to ensure the prediction performance in this situation. As is shown in the flow chart,
after a successful fast reacquisition, if the target light is lost again in X seconds from the
beginning time of prediction, the system will go back to the latest valid prediction and
continue to execute the predicted pointing angle. This method helps to take full use of the
effective tracking data and the prediction results. In addition, to ensure a minimum valid data

5. Summary

In this paper, the validity of predictive filtering based on the angle information was analyzed,
and an optimized prediction algorithm was designed based on the finite memory filter.
Simulation results indicated good prediction performance of the algorithm, and laboratory
acquisition experiments further demonstrated the effectiveness of the fast reacquisition
approach in an actual ATP system. Achieving rapid reacquisition is important for improving
the average data rate and reducing the beam power requirement in laser communication
applications.

To further shorten the reacquisition time, more advanced design of reacquisition approach,
such as combining the predictive filtering and scanning methods, should be studied in the
future. And more experiments that simulate the real-life application scenarios should be
conducted to validate the approach.
