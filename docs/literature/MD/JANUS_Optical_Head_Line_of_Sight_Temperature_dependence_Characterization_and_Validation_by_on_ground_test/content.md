Abstract— JANUS (Jupiter Amorum ac Natorum Undique
Scrutator) is the multispectral camera that, on board of JUICE
(JUpiter ICy moons Explorer) ESA mission, will provide high-
resolution images of Jupiter atmosphere and major Galilean
icy moons surface: Europa, Ganymede, Callisto. Inertial
pointing, mosaicking, object tracking, stereo imaging for DTM
(Digital Terrain Model) and the peculiar Ganymede Libration
measurements defined highly stringent requirement on the LoS
(Line of Sight) knowledge of the JANUS OHU (Optical Head
Unit). Once in flight, the JANUS LoS is expected to vary as
consequence of thermal environment variations, in fact, orbit
attitude change, spacecraft maneuvering and scientific
payloads heat dissipations can modify the OHU temperature
distribution. Optical elements translation and rotation have
been predicted through a STOP (Structural Thermal Optical
and Performance) Analysis, deriving a linear relation between
LoS, value and uncertainty, and OHU structure temperature
gradient. This relation has been validated thorough an on-
ground and in-air characterization test, the results are
presented in this paper. Test based coefficients allow real time
and indirect LoS prediction & evaluation as function of OHU
temperature sensor readings.

Keywords— JUICE ESA mission, JANUS, Optical stability,
Performances, Line of Sight Space Instrument, High resolution
camera

survey the Jovian system with a special focus on the three
Galilean Moons — Europa, Ganymede and Callisto —
becoming the first SpaceCraft (S/C) to orbit around the
Jupiter moon Ganymede [1]. The main objective of JUICE is
to address two key questions: what are the conditions for
planet formation and the emergence of life? And how does
the Solar System work? The main components of the mission
scientific strategy are: 1) to conduct a comparative study of

Il. LOS CHARACTERIZATION WITH IN-AIR TEST

The test focused on the main contributor of the LoS, that
is the OMS (Opto Mechanical Structure) [3] deformation due
to BW (Baffle Wall) and OW (Optical Wall) temperature
gradient.

According to [2], Y axis rotation is dominant wrt (with
respect to) X rotation, for this reason:

LoS = LoSy                          ①⑪

Knowing that all other sources of LoS variation are of
negligible effect, the test was conducted using the OHU
STM (Structural Thermal Model), including FPM (Focal
Plane Module) STM and FWM (Filter Wheel Module) STM.
External harness and the S/C purging line were not installed.

A. Concept

Installed on an aluminum rigid interface plate (kept at
ambient temperature) the OW was heated to obtain a
temperature gradient wrt the BW. This gradient leaded to a
differential contraction in X direction of the OW wrt BW,
indeed causing a flexure of the full OMS (i.e. Y rotation).

The temperature difference between OW and BW is
named: Wall’s temperature gradient (AT wats).

Fig. 1. : OHU LoS variation due to Walls temperature gradient (spatial)

B. Assumption
・ Primary Mirror tilt = LoS of OHU

The LoS main contribution is the global tilting of
OHU structure. Being TEM (Telescope Module)
stiffly connected to the OW, any rotation of the OW
causes TEM optical components rotation. So, the
measurement of the MI (Primary Mirror) rotation is
equivalent to measure the whole TEM rotation, and
of all OHU LoS variation.

* Air environment and convection effects

Involving only the reflection
measurement, the test was done in air.

for theodolite

The advantages of testing in air were: a) the constant
accessibility to the OHU and the setup; b) the short
transitory of the temperature distribution accelerated
thanks to convection; c) the absence of «chamber
windows bending» due to vacuum that affect the
theodolite measurement uncertainty (the LoS
variation was previously tested in vacuum, but the
depressurized condition induced the window to bend;
such bending introduced uncertainty during
measurement of more than 60 arcsec, larger than the
measure itself).

The disadvantage were: a) the convection constantly
chilling the OHU so heating power to be injected was
widely lost (ic. setup required high Wattage); b)
convection enhanced the hot spots location due to
heating injection and reduced the uniformity of the
temperature distribution.

・ Temperature stability in air similar to in flight

The test data were collected when the variation of the
MI temperature wrt the OW temperature was slower

than 2°C/h. From OHU in flight thermal analysis
(Ganymede 500km orbit), the variation is predicted to
be 1°C/h.

So, the collection of data during in air-lab test was
done when thermal transient was comparable and any
eventual different elongation, that could have induced
a relative rotation between MI and OW, was
considered negligible wrt global OMS angular
rotation (confirmed by analysis [2]).

・ Heating source location representativeness

Test heaters installed were sufficiently representative
of the hot spots expected from thermal analysis, see
Fig. 4.

* Interface

OHU interface was rigidly fixed on an aluminum
plate kept at room temperature to avoid distortion due
to differential thermo-elastic elongation. The plate
needed to be monitored to check its constant
temperature all along the test. No external harnesses
were installed.

* Theodolite measurement

The rotation of the MI was measured with a
Theodolite, stable and permanently fixed on the lab
floor. Since STM flat mirror was uncoated, an
Optical Cube was glued on its surface, see Fig. 3.

B. Proportionality Factor

Adopting the proportional relation between LoS and
AT wats in agreement with the multidisciplinary study in (2),
the K proportional factor was calculated as best linear fit of
the test data. The fit was imposed to pass for the reference
measurement at ambient temperature, where by definition the
LoS is zero when no temperature gradient is present on the
structure. And the uncertainty calculation of the K factor is
obtained as well as linear fit of the measurements including
uncertainty values.

The fit shows a partial agreement of the test
measurements at low ATwatss, the causes is probably the not
completion of the transitory (ie. the temperature of the
structure was rising slowly, not stabilized yet, causing low
distortion being still close to ambient temperature).

A, Walls gradient measurement during flight

When OHU will be in space, during imaging while
orbiting, the measurements of the Wall’s temperature will be
recorded by two PT1000 in specific locations of the box, see
Fig. 9.

Fig. 9. : Location of PT1000 in OHU Proto Flight Model

To estimate the uncertainty of the Walls gradient
measurement using only two sensors (i.e. the temperature
measurment might be representative only of the local
position), OHU STM TVC (Thermal Vacuum Cycling) test
data were port-processed. A wide set of PT100 sensors (at
different Walls locations) were registered, offering a direct
evaluation of the Walls temperature homogeneity. During
TVC, thanks to the absence of convection and the slow
boundary condition change, the gradient based on sensors
BW-X and OW-X (see Fig. 9 and10 to appreciate the
identical location of PT100 and PT 1000) resulted very close
to the value estimated by averaging all available sensors.

In Fig. 11, the difference between the gradient measurement
methods (averiging of all sensors available versus use of only
two, like on PFM) is represented by a green line. The
maximum and the minimum peak occurred in
correspondence of the STM vacuum test boundaries switch
(fast change from/to hot/cold non operative environments).
These unrealistic variation will never be seen in flight
(slower time scale of Spacecraft Attitude and Orbit changes
during JANUS imaging) High temperature uniformity of
OW and BW are expected. The uncertainty is assumed at 1-
sigma as the standard deviation over the whole temperature
history recoreded in STM TVC test.

V. CONCLUSIONS

Thanks to the in air characterization of the LoS as
function of the Wall's gradient, the proportional constant K
has been measured and expressed with its uncertainty.

From the data analysis collected during OHU STM
thermal balance/cycle test, the error due to the PT1000
position representativeness wrt average of Walls temperature
has been derived and considered for the uncertainty
estimation.

As result, when OHU will be in flight, the scientific team
can use the telemetry of the two PT1000 sensors embedded
in OHU structure (at Baffle Wall and Optical Wall location)
to have a real time indirect measurement of the LoS and the
AKE.
